\begin{abstract}
We construct one explicit smooth compactly supported Hamiltonian moving arbitrarily placed disjoint closed source
balls into an open target ball in two regimes. In dimension four it realises every packing with
$\sum_i c_i^2<C^2$ and $3c_i<C$ for every $i$. This gives a new proof of Biran's full-packing theorem for every
$k\ge9$ equal balls, without $J$-holomorphic curves or Seiberg--Witten theory. In dimensions $2n\ge6$ it realises
every packing permitted by the volume and Gromov two-ball inequalities, turning the Siegel--Yao existence criterion,
proved in a 2026 preprint of OpenAI, into an explicit Hamiltonian motion.
One uniform halting oracle algorithm outputs a finite rational polynomial times a fixed smooth cutoff, certified to
give the same packing. The construction combines finite rational comparison iterates and explicit planar convex
rearrangements, finite-atlas Moser flows and explicit returns from cubic and diagonal-quadric models, and rational
trajectory certificates for the finite output. The companion paper gives a shorter route for $k\ge10$ equal balls
in dimension four using a linear comparison identity. The main geometric and algorithmic theorems are verified in
Lean~4.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Authorship and the role of AI}

The manuscripts, mathematical proofs, Lean~4 formalisation and accompanying programs of this release were generated by
AI models, including GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI), Claude Opus~5.5 (Anthropic), Gemini~4 Carbon (Google) and other models.
Liang Song chose the problem, designed and directed the research environment, organised cross-model review and takes
sole responsibility for the manuscript and its release.

Trustworthiness rests on explicit verification layers: the main theorems of Papers~I--II (including the uniform
finite-output algorithm and perturbed fixed-step RK4 target containment) are checked in Lean~4 against an independent
specification importing only Mathlib, using only the three standard axioms and re-verified by fresh kernel replay
(Section~\ref{p2:sec-ver}); Papers~III--IV give human-readable proofs supported by multi-model cross-review and
numerical checks; and program outputs serve only as numerical corroboration.
