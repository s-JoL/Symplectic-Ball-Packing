\begin{abstract}
We realise symplectic ball packings by explicit Hamiltonian motions. For admissible data one definition gives a
smooth compactly supported Hamiltonian $H[n,k,C,c,s]$ whose time-one map carries the closed balls $B(s_i,c_i)$, at
arbitrary disjoint positions, into the open ball of capacity $C$ with pairwise disjoint images; one algorithm outputs
a Hamiltonian of the form (fixed cutoff)$\times$(rational polynomial) with the same property. In dimension $2n\ge6$ the
admissible data are those satisfying the volume and Gromov two-ball inequalities, so every packing allowed by
Conjecture~A of Siegel and Yao, proved in a 2026 preprint of OpenAI, is realised by an explicit flow. In dimension four
they are the data with $\sum_ic_i^2<C^2$ and $3c_i<C$; in particular $k\ge9$ equal balls fill $B^4$ up to any
$\eps>0$, which proves Biran's theorem without $J$-holomorphic curves or Seiberg--Witten theory.
The companion paper treats $k\ge10$ equal balls in dimension four by a linear comparison function. The new
ingredients here are a comparison datum for every concave profile, given by a finite rational iterate of a
rearrangement contraction; a scalar comparison datum for the four-dimensional cubic model; an explicit
area-preserving rearrangement of planar convex functions, lifted to toric fibres; a bordered handle whose Moser maps are
flows of explicit fields in a finite atlas; a model over the diagonal curve $y_j^2=t^2-j$ with an explicit transfer to
the ball; for one large ball, a closed-form shell return ($n\ge4$) and a polynomial bubble ($n=3$); and a sound
rational certificate for the finite output. The main results are verified in Lean~4.
\end{abstract}
