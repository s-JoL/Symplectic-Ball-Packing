\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The result}\label{sec:intro-problem}

Points of $\R^{2n}=\C^n$ are $z=(z_1,\dots,z_n)$, $z_j=x_j+iy_j$, with symplectic form
$\omega_0=\sum_jdx_j\wedge dy_j$. The Hamiltonian vector field of a function $H(t,z)$ is defined by
$\iota_{X_H}\omega_0=dH$, i.e.\ $X_H=(\partial_{y}H,-\partial_{x}H)$, and $\phi^t_H$ denotes its flow from time $0$ to
time $t$. We write $B(c)=\{\pi|z|^2\le c\}$ for the closed ball of capacity $c$, $\Bo(c)$ for its interior, and
$B(s,c)=s+B(c)$. Thus $B(s,\pi)$ is the closed unit ball about $s$, and $\Bo(\pi R^2)$ is the open Euclidean ball of
radius $R$ about the origin.

\begin{maintheorem}[Equal balls in the four-ball]\label{thm:intro-4d}
\uses{def:ham-H-final, def:intro-spec, prop:cmp-linear, prop:ham-embeddings, prop:ham-end}
Let $k\ge10$ be an integer and $\eps\in(0,1)$. Put
\[
 R=\left(\frac{k}{1-\eps/4}\right)^{1/4},\qquad C=\pi R^2,\qquad s_i=(3i-3,0,0,0)\in\R^4\quad(1\le i\le k),
\]
and $H_{k,\eps}=H[2,k,C,(\pi,\dots,\pi),(s_1,\dots,s_k)]$ (Definition~\ref{def:ham-H-final}). Then $H_{k,\eps}$ is
$C^\infty$ on $\R\times\R^4$, vanishes for $t\notin(0,1)$ and outside $\R\times\mathcal S$ for an explicit compact set
$\mathcal S$, and satisfies $\Pack(2,k,C,(\pi,\dots,\pi),s)$ (Definition~\ref{def:intro-spec}): its flow is global, and
its time-one map sends each closed unit ball $B(s_i,\pi)$ into $\Bo(\pi R^2)$, with pairwise disjoint images. Moreover
$k/R^4=1-\eps/4>1-\eps$.
\leanmark{four\_dim\_theorem, main\_time\_support, main\_support\_radius, flow\_images\_disjoint}
\end{maintheorem}

\begin{proof}
The centres are at distance at least $3$, so the closed unit balls are disjoint. Since $R^4=k/(1-\eps/4)>k\ge10$, we
have $k\pi^2<\pi^2R^4=C^2$ and $3\pi<\pi R^2=C$, so $(2,k,C,(\pi,\dots,\pi))$ is admissible
(Definition~\ref{def:intro-admissible}). The normalised capacity $\rho=\pi/C=R^{-2}$ satisfies
$\rho\sqrt k=\sqrt{1-\eps/4}>\sqrt3/2>\frac34$, so the data lie in the linear branch, which uses the linear
comparison datum of Proposition~\ref{prop:cmp-linear}. The proof is completed in \S\ref{sec:ham-proof-main}.
\end{proof}

The Hamiltonian is defined by the same formulas for all $(k,\eps)$. It is built from closed formulas, integrals,
time-one maps of explicit vector fields, inverses of explicit injective maps and extrema over explicit compact sets,
the operations of level L1 in Definition~\ref{def:intro-levels} (Table~\ref{tab:ham-explicit}).

The companion paper~\cite{SongHD} defines $H[n,k,C,c,s]$ for all admissible data and proves the packing for unequal
capacities in dimension four ($\sum_ic_i^2<C^2$, $3c_i<C$), for $k=9$ equal balls and in every dimension $2n\ge6$; it
also gives a uniform algorithm whose output is a rational polynomial times a fixed cutoff. The two papers supersede
the single paper~\cite{SongAll}.

\emph{The Epoch problem.} Epoch AI \cite{Epoch} asked for an algorithm that, on input $k\ge10$ and $\eps\in(0,1)$,
outputs an explicit smooth Hamiltonian on $\R^4$ whose time-one map sends the closed unit balls centred at
$(3i-3,0,0,0)$ into a closed ball $B^4(R)$ about the origin with $k/R^4>1-\eps$. Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-4d},
Proposition~\ref{thm:intro-stability} and Corollary~\ref{cor:rk4-containment} answer the mathematical part of this
construction problem and prove eventual containment for fixed-step RK4; the algorithm with finite output is
in~\cite{SongHD}. Epoch also asks for a PyTorch implementation meeting published \texttt{float64} interface and runtime
limits, which remains a separate implementation objective. Two accompanying programs in \texttt{program/} explore
complementary computational routes: one in Epoch's Hamiltonian interface built from the elementary constructions of the
third paper of the release~\cite{SongP3} over a covered range of $(k,\eps)$, and one evaluating closed-form $C^4$
packing embeddings and exact-real inverse certificates directly for every $k\ge10$ and $\eps\in(0,1)$, proved in the
fourth paper of the release~\cite{SongP4}.

\subsection{What was known}\label{sec:intro-prior}

Balls of capacities $c_1,\dots,c_k$ embed disjointly in $\Bo(C)\subset\R^4$ only if $\sum_ic_i^2<C^2$ and, by Gromov's
two-ball theorem \cite{Gromov1985}, $c_i+c_j<C$. McDuff and Polterovich \cite{McDuffPolterovich1994} related such
packings to blow-ups of $\CP^2$ and found the maximal densities for at most nine equal balls; Biran
\cite{Biran1997} proved that $k$ balls of capacity $c$ embed in $\Bo(C)$ whenever $k\ge9$ and $kc^2<C^2$; and the work
of McDuff, Li--Liu and Li--Li \cite{McDuff1991,McDuff1998,LiLiu2001,LiLi2002} reduces every four-dimensional ball
packing problem to inequalities indexed by exceptional classes. Packing stability was extended by Biran \cite{Biran1999}, Buse, Hind and Opshtein
\cite{BuseHind2011,BuseHind2013,BuseHindOpshtein2016} and Edtmair \cite{Edtmair2025}, and fails for some open domains
\cite{CristofaroGardinerHind2023}. These proofs use $J$-holomorphic curves and, through Taubes' theorem and inflation,
Seiberg--Witten theory; they show that embeddings exist but do not produce a Hamiltonian moving given balls.
Classical explicit full packings include the square-number constructions of Traynor \cite{Traynor1995} and Schlenk
\cite{SchlenkHand,SchlenkBook,Schlenk2018}; other constructive approaches include folding \cite{SchlenkFolding},
tunnelling \cite{Wieck2009}, constructions from singular symplectic curves and polarisations \cite{Opshtein2011,Opshtein2013},
and almost toric methods \cite{Symington2003,CasalsVianna2020}. To our knowledge, the present work gives the first
uniform explicit construction of a smooth compactly supported Hamiltonian moving the prescribed source balls to
arbitrarily dense packings for every non-square $k\ge10$, within one family valid for all $k\ge10$ and every positive
volume deficit. The construction and its finite-output algorithm~\cite{SongHD} do not invoke $J$-holomorphic curves,
Seiberg--Witten theory, or prior packing-existence theorems. The OpenAI preprint \cite{OpenAI2026} proved the packing
criterion conjectured by Siegel and Yao \cite{SiegelYao2025} in dimensions $2n\ge6$; in dimension four the two
inequalities are not a criterion. We use its twisted disc-bundle mechanism (whose comparison and surface lemmas allow
fibres $\C^m$, $m\ge1$) and the deformation-to-the-normal-cone scheme of its return. Dimension four needs a different
model and new arguments: the Fermat cubic disc bundle, the linear comparison identity, which replaces the fixed point by
a closed formula, and the symmetry argument that brings the model back to the ball.

The companion paper~\cite{SongHD} adds the scalar comparison datum, which covers $k=9$ and unequal capacities, and so
proves in the same way Biran's theorem \cite{Biran1997} that $B^4$ admits full packings by $k$ equal balls for every
$k\ge9$; the earlier proofs \cite{Biran1997,Biran1999,BuseHind2013,BuseHindOpshtein2016} use $J$-holomorphic curves and
Seiberg--Witten theory, directly or through the results they cite.

\subsection{The key ideas}\label{sec:intro-mechanism}

The model is Biran's decomposition of $\CP^2$ along a smooth cubic $E$ \cite{Biran2001}: the complement of an
isotropic skeleton is the degree-$9$ disc bundle over $E$ with fibres of capacity $\frac13$, and its base area at
fibre moment $p$ is $A(p)=3(1-3p)$. Balls are placed fibrewise; for $k\ge10$ balls of capacity close to $1/\sqrt k$ the
volume budget holds but the slice profile $\sum_i(a_i-p)_+$ exceeds $A(p)$ near $p=0$ (Figure~\ref{fig:intro-profile}).
Following \cite{OpenAI2026}, the bundle is twisted across an annulus in the base by an area-preserving map $q$ of the
fibre disc. Paired with a function $K$ of the moment, the twist moves base area between moment levels, and the balls fit
if, for $P=A-\sum_i(a_i-p)_+$ and some gap $\gamma>0$,
\[
 (K-P)\bigl(p(q(w))\bigr)\le K\bigl(p(w)\bigr)-\gamma\qquad\text{for every fibre point }w
\]
(Section~\ref{sec:mech}). In \cite{OpenAI2026} the function $K$ is the fixed point of a contraction built from
increasing rearrangements, and the planar maps realising the rearrangements and the relative Moser isotopies are
obtained from existence statements. Two new four-dimensional ingredients make the entire construction explicit and free
of iterative fixed points.

\emph{1.\ The linear comparison identity} (\S\ref{sec:cmp-linear}). For $k\ge10$ equal balls of capacity $a=t_0/\sqrt k$,
with the bundle cut at fibre moment $h=t_0/3$, the function $K-P$ with $K(p)=(3\sqrt k-9)p$ linear is, up to the
constant $-g$, a positive multiple of the generally asymmetric tent with vertex at $p=a$ and endpoint values
$\Lambda(0)=\Lambda(h)=h$: its slopes are $-A=-h/a$ and $B=h/(h-a)$. Since $1/A+1/B=1$,
each sublevel set $\{\Lambda\le b\}$ ($0\le b\le h$) has Lebesgue
measure exactly $b$, so the increasing rearrangement of $\Lambda$ on $[0,h]$ is the identity function and the comparison
inequality reduces algebraically to the constant gap $g=3(1-t_0)>0$. One explicit planar area-preserving isotopy realises
the rearrangement up to radial smoothing at a cost of at most $g/32$, leaving gap $\frac{31}{32}g$ with no contraction
fixed point.

\emph{2.\ The Fermat cubic disc bundle and $\Z/3$-symmetric return} (\S\ref{sec:mdl-cub}, \S\ref{sec:mdl-return}). The
degree-$9$ normal disc bundle of the Fermat cubic $Z_0^3+Z_1^3+Z_2^3=0$ is written explicitly by theta functions on the
universal cover $\R^2\times\C$ of the Fermat torus and carried into $\CP^2$ by explicit normal-bundle injection,
deformation-to-the-normal-cone transport, and K\"ahler Moser flows. Since $\Bo(1)$ is $\CP^2$ minus the line
$L_\infty=\{Z_0=0\}$, the ball images must avoid $L_\infty$. Every map of the return commutes with the order-$3$
projective symmetry $[Z_0:Z_1:Z_2]\mapsto[e^{2\pi i/3}Z_0:Z_1:Z_2]$, whose fixed locus is $L_\infty$ together with
$[1:0:0]$. A point mapped into $L_\infty$ is therefore fixed by the $\Z/3$-action on the model, hence comes from one of
the three marked fibres $\{Z_0=0\}\cap E$, and the handle placement routes all $k$ balls into the complement of those
three fibres. Avoidance of $L_\infty$ thus follows exact-algebraically from $\Z/3$ equivariance rather than from a $C^0$
Moser bound.

\subsection{Five-step route and verification guarantees}\label{sec:intro-organisation}

Section~\ref{sec:results} states the packing specification, the vector-field robustness estimate, the Epoch data, and
uniform target containment for perturbed fixed-step RK4. After rescaling by $1/\sqrt C$, the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-4d} proceeds in five self-contained steps:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Model} (\S\ref{sec:mech}, \S\ref{sec:mdl-cub}): construct the explicit symplectic model of the degree-$9$
cubic disc bundle on $\R^2\times\C$, cut at fibre moment $p\le h=t_0/3$, with three $\Z/3$-fixed marked points on the
Fermat torus.
\item \emph{Comparison} (Section~\ref{sec:cmp}): apply the linear comparison identity $K(p)=(3\sqrt k-9)p$ and construct
an explicit compactly supported fibre isotopy $q_t$ on $\Bo(h)$ achieving comparison gap $\frac{31}{32}g$.
\item \emph{Handle} (Section~\ref{sec:hdl}): twist the disc bundle over a base annulus avoiding the three marked fibres
and compose three explicit Moser flows to embed the $k$ balls into disjoint horizontal layers of the model.
\item \emph{Return} (Section~\ref{sec:mdl}): transport the model symplectically into $\CP^2\setminus L_\infty\cong\Bo(1)$
via $\Z/3$-equivariant normal-cone and K\"ahler Moser flows; Table~\ref{tab:mdl-margins} tracks the explicit quantitative
margins passed from step to step.
\item \emph{Motion} (Section~\ref{sec:ham}): connect the standard source balls $B(s_i,\pi)$ to the resulting symplectic
embeddings in $\Bo(\pi R^2)$ by a single compactly supported $C^\infty$ Alexander--polar Hamiltonian isotopy whose $k$
collars remain pairwise disjoint for all $t\in[0,1]$.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{What guarantees each part.} Every step above is given both as a human-readable mathematical proof (outlined in
Sections~\ref{sec:mech}--\ref{sec:ham} with full analytic details in the supplement, prefixed by~S as in
\Ssec{sec:pll}) and as a machine-checked proof in Lean~4 (Section~\ref{sec:ver}). In \texttt{verification/Problem.lean},
written from Mathlib alone, $\Pack$ requires global $C^\infty$ smoothness, uniform compact spatial support, existence of
global trajectories, and target containment for \emph{every} integral curve starting in \emph{every} source ball; Lean
checks Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-4d}, Proposition~\ref{thm:intro-stability}, and Corollaries~\ref{thm:main-epoch}--\ref{cor:rk4-containment}
for the named Hamiltonian $H_{k,\eps}$ using only the three standard axioms (\texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}), re-verified from scratch by fresh kernel replay, while metaprogram
audits check syntactic explicitness and independence from prior packing-existence or $J$-holomorphic curve theorems. The
companion Paper~II~\cite{SongHD} extends the formalised proof to $k=9$, unequal capacities, dimensions $2n\ge6$, and a
halting rational polynomial-cutoff algorithm; Paper~III proves elementary toric/window constructions and density
obstructions (human-readable proofs plus exact rational checks); Paper~IV proves ODE-free closed-form $C^4$ packing maps
and exact-real inverse certificates (human-readable proofs corroborated by \texttt{float64} and 100-digit
\texttt{mpmath} checks, which are not used inside any proof).

\emph{Conventions.}\label{sec:intro-conventions} The dilation $z\mapsto\lambda z$ multiplies $\omega_0$ and
capacities by $\lambda^2$. In fibre coordinates $w_j$ the moments are $p_j=\pi|w_j|^2$ and the angles
$\theta_j=\arg w_j/2\pi\in\R/\Z$, so the fibre form is $\omega_V=\sum_jdp_j\wedge d\theta_j$. Fibre Hamiltonians $Q$
follow the sign of \cite{OpenAI2026}, $\iota_{X_Q}\omega_V=-dQ$, opposite to the global one. In affine coordinates
$\omega_{\rm FS}=dd^c\log(1+|z|^2)$ with $d^c=i(\bar\partial-\partial)/(4\pi)$; the map $z\mapsto z/\sqrt{\pi(1+|z|^2)}$
identifies this chart symplectically with $\Bo(1)$.
